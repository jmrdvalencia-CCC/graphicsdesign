Part 1 • 25 Minutes
What is Typography?
The art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and visually appealing. It communicates not just what is said, but how it feels.
🗣
Communication
Delivers the written message clearly and efficiently — a headline readable at a glance
🎭
Expression
Conveys tone, personality, and emotion through letterform — before a word is read
🏗
Structure
Organizes information into a visual hierarchy — headline, deck, byline, body
💡 Key Concept: Typography is never neutral. Every typeface choice sends a message before a single word is read.
Terminology
Typeface vs Font vs Typography
Three terms constantly confused — understanding the distinction is essential for professional communication.
TermDefinitionExample
TypefaceThe overall design of a set of letterforms — the familyPlayfair Display
FontA specific instance at a particular weight and stylePlayfair Display Bold Italic
TypographyThe practice of arranging type in a design — the craftChoosing, sizing, spacing, placing type
💬 Analogy: A typeface is like a song. A font is like a specific recording (acoustic, live, remix). Typography is the act of curating a playlist.

Font Pairing
Selecting two or more typefaces that work together harmoniously — contrast creates interest, shared quality creates cohesion.

🌟 The Golden Rule: Fonts should be different enough to create contrast, but similar enough to feel like they belong together.

Assessment • Short Answer
Check Your Understanding
Question 01
What is the difference between a typeface, a font, and typography?
Tap for answer →
Answer
Typeface = the family design. Font = specific weight/style instance. Typography = the craft of arranging type in a layout.
Question 02
A designer pairs Lobster + Pacifico for a restaurant menu. What is wrong?
Tap for answer →
Answer
Two script fonts — both compete, both illegible at body size. Fix: one script headline + a clean sans-serif body (e.g. Open Sans).
Question 03
Difference between tracking and kerning? When to use each?
Tap for answer →
Answer
Tracking = uniform spacing across all characters. Kerning = spacing between specific pairs. Use tracking for ALL CAPS labels; kerning for logos and display headlines.
Question 04
A body text column has 110 characters per line. What is the problem?
Tap for answer →
Answer
Too wide — the eye loses its place returning to the next line. Fix: narrow the column to 45–75 characters per line.
