Part 1 Design Elements & Principles Overview
Learning Objectives

What You Will Learn

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

  • 1Identify and describe the basic elements and principles of design.
  • 2Define and explain the concept of balance in graphic design.
  • 3Distinguish between the three types of balance: symmetrical, asymmetrical, and radial.
  • 4Apply balance principles in evaluating and creating design compositions.
1.1 — Design Elements

What Are Design Elements?

Click each card to reveal the description. (Mrantinkovic et al., 2020)

Line
Tap to reveal →
Connects two points; creates direction, movement, and structure.
📌 The curved ribbon lines in the Coca-Cola logo.
💭 What feeling does a diagonal line give vs. a horizontal line?
Shape
Tap to reveal →
A 2D enclosed area defined by lines or color boundaries.
📌 The smile/arrow shape in the Amazon logo.
💭 Why do circles feel friendlier than sharp triangles?
Color
Tap to reveal →
Hue is the color itself. Saturation is its intensity. Value is its lightness or darkness.
📌 Coca-Cola's high-saturation red creates urgency and energy.
💭 How would Coca-Cola feel if its red were replaced with pale pink?
Texture
Tap to reveal →
The visual or tactile surface quality of a design element.
📌 A rough stone texture in a poster vs. a smooth gradient background.
💭 Can texture exist in a digital design? How?
Space
Tap to reveal →
Area around, between, or within elements — both positive and negative space.
📌 The generous white space around the Apple logo signals premium quality.
💭 What happens to a design that has no negative space at all?
Form
Tap to reveal →
3D representation of shape — has height, width, and depth.
📌 A product package box or a 3D rendered logo with shadows and highlights.
💭 How does adding depth (form) change how a viewer perceives a flat shape?
Value
Tap to reveal →
The lightness or darkness of a color or tone in a composition.
📌 Samsung's deep navy blue has a low value, conveying authority and trust.
💭 How does value create the illusion of depth in a flat design?
Typography
Tap to reveal →
The style, arrangement, and appearance of text in a design.
📌 Amazon's bold lowercase font feels approachable; a serif font feels formal.
💭 If you changed a logo's font, would it still feel like the same brand?
💬 Identify design elements in each logo below. Which elements stand out most?
1
Emphasis — The bold red color and large script text immediately draw the viewer’s eye to the brand name.
2
Contrast — White text on red background creates strong contrast for readability and visual impact.
3
Unity — The consistent use of red, white, and the script font creates a cohesive, unified identity.

1.2 — Design Principles

What Are Design Principles?

Hover over each card to learn more. (Mrantinkovic et al., 2020)

Balance
Equal visual weight distribution.
Contrast
Difference to create visual interest.
Emphasis
Drawing attention to a focal point.
Movement
Guiding the viewer's eye.
Repetition
Repeating elements for consistency.
Proportion
Size relationship between elements.
Unity
All elements working as a whole.
White Space
Strategic use of empty space.
