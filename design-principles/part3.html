Part 3 Application & Analysis
3.1 — Group Activity

Design Critique

Analyzing balance in real design samples

📋 Instructions: Divide the class into 3 groups. Each group receives a printed or projected design sample and analyzes it using the questions below.
Group 1
⚖️ Symmetrical Design
Analyze a corporate logo or formal poster. Identify the axis of symmetry and how mirror elements create stability.
Group 2
🎨 Asymmetrical Design
Analyze a magazine cover or web layout. Identify how different elements achieve equal visual weight without mirroring.
Group 3
🌀 Radial Design
Analyze a mandala or circular poster. Identify the central focal point and how elements radiate outward.

Each Group Answers:

  • 1What type of balance is used in this design?
  • 2What elements contribute to the visual weight?
  • 3Does the design feel balanced? Why or why not?
  • 4How would you improve the balance if needed?
3.2 — Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes in Balance

What to avoid when designing for balance

⚠️
Heavy elements on one side — Placing all large or dark elements on one side without any compensation on the other creates an unstable, uncomfortable composition.
⚠️
Too many competing focal points — When multiple elements fight for attention equally, the viewer's eye has no clear path and the design feels chaotic.
⚠️
Ignoring negative/white space — Empty space is a powerful balancing element. Failing to use it intentionally leads to cluttered, unbalanced layouts.
⚠️
Over-relying on symmetry — Using only symmetrical balance when asymmetry would be more effective results in designs that feel rigid and uninspired.
⚠️
Forgetting color and texture weight — A small dark element can outweigh a large light one. Ignoring this leads to unintended visual imbalance.
