Part 2 Balance in Graphic Design
2.1 — Definition

What Is Balance?

The equal distribution of visual weight within a composition. (Mrantinkovic et al., 2020)

Balance is the principle of design that refers to the equal distribution of visual weight within a composition. It creates a sense of stability, harmony, and order.

Visual weight is determined by:

📏
Size
Larger elements feel heavier.
🎨
Color
Dark or saturated colors feel heavier.
🪨
Texture
Textured areas attract more attention.
📍
Position
Elements far from center carry more weight.
🔢
Quantity
Many small elements can balance one large one.
🔑 Key Concept: Balance does NOT mean everything must be equal in size or placement — it means the overall composition feels visually stable.
2.2A — Types of Balance

Symmetrical Balance

Also called Formal Balance

Formal / Symmetrical

Elements on both sides of a central axis are mirror images of each other.

  • Creates a sense of formality, stability, and elegance
  • Used in: logos, formal invitations, government seals, religious art
  • Can feel static or rigid if overused
  • Example: butterfly wings, human face, Taj Mahal facade

✏️ Activity: Show 3 examples of symmetrical designs. Ask students to identify the axis of symmetry.

2.2B — Types of Balance

Asymmetrical Balance

Also called Informal Balance

Informal / Asymmetrical

Different elements on each side have equal visual weight but are not mirror images.

  • Creates a sense of dynamism, energy, and modernity
  • More complex and visually interesting
  • Used in: magazine layouts, web design, modern advertising

How to achieve it:

  • Large element balanced by several small elements
  • Dark color balanced by a larger light-colored element
  • Textured area balanced by large white space

✏️ Activity: Sketch an asymmetrical layout using basic shapes (circle, square, triangle). Share and discuss.

2.2C — Types of Balance

Radial Balance

Elements radiate outward from a central point

Radial

Elements radiate outward from a central point, like spokes on a wheel.

  • Creates a sense of movement, energy, and focus
  • The center becomes the natural focal point
  • Used in: mandalas, sunburst designs, circular logos, clock faces
  • Example: sunflower, dartboard, compass rose

💬 Discussion: Where do you commonly see radial balance in everyday life? (Wheels, flowers, pizza slices, etc.)

2.4 — Visual Weight

Balance & Visual Weight

Understanding what makes elements feel "heavy" or "light"

1
Size
A large shape appears heavier than a small one.
2
Color
Warm, dark, or saturated colors feel heavier.
3
Isolation
An element surrounded by space draws more attention.
4
Complexity
Detailed or textured elements feel heavier.
5
Position
Elements at the top or edges feel heavier.
"A designer must develop an intuitive sense of visual weight to create compositions that feel naturally balanced without being rigid."
— Mrantinkovic et al. (2020), 3G Handy Guide: Graphic Designing
