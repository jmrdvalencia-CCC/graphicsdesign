Part 4 · Section 4.1

Why Scalability is Non-Negotiable

A logo appears in an enormous range of sizes throughout its life. A raster logo at 500×500 px is unusable at billboard scale. A vector logo reproduces perfectly at all sizes.

ApplicationApproximate Size
Favicon (browser tab)16 × 16 px to 32 × 32 px
Social media profile picture400 × 400 px
Website header120–300 px wide
Business card15–25 mm wide
Letterhead30–50 mm wide
Signage / storefront300–1,000 mm wide
Vehicle wrap500–2,000 mm wide
Billboard3,000–14,000 mm wide

The Core Principle

Scalability is a core requirement of professional graphic design output. A design that cannot be reproduced consistently across media has failed a fundamental professional standard.

Key Concept Color is an enhancement, not a crutch. The strongest logos — Nike, Apple, McDonald's arches — are instantly recognizable as pure black silhouettes. Build the form first; add color second.
Part 4 · Section 4.2

The Scalability Test

Before finalizing any logo, apply this two-part test.

Test 1 — Minimum Size

Display the logo at 15 mm / 57 px wide. Ask:

  • Is the brand name still readable?
  • Are all symbol details still visible?
  • Does the overall shape still read clearly?

If any answer is no — simplify until it passes.

Test 2 — One-Color Silhouette

Fill the entire logo with solid black. Remove all gradients and color. Ask:

  • Is the logo still recognizable?
  • Does the shape communicate the brand without color?
  • Would it work embroidered or engraved?

If any answer is no — the form is too dependent on color.

Critical Rule The .AI file is the master. All other formats are exports. If the AI file is lost, the logo must be rebuilt from scratch.
