Part 5 · Section 6.1

Key Takeaways

  • A logo is the visual symbol of a brand — not the brand itself. Its power comes from the story and reputation it represents over time.
  • The five logo types: wordmark, lettermark, brandmark, combination mark, and emblem. A combination mark is the most versatile starting point.
  • An effective logo is simple, memorable, timeless, versatile, and appropriate — functional requirements, not aesthetic preferences.
  • Sketching before software is the professional standard. The thumbnail grid (20 rough → 5 refined → 2–3 to digitize) produces better concepts faster.
  • Line and shape are the primary design elements in logo sketching. Their character communicates personality before color or typography are applied.
  • Logos must be built as vector graphics (AI, EPS, SVG) — not raster images. Vector paths scale to any size without quality loss.
  • The Pen tool is the primary digitizing tool. Mastery requires using the fewest anchor points possible.
  • Complete delivery: AI (master), EPS (print), SVG (web), PNG (general use) + all color variations.
  • Every logo must pass the scalability test: readable at 15 mm wide and recognizable as a one-color black silhouette.
Part 5 · Sections 6.2 & 6.3

Assessment & Comparison

§ 6.2 — Short Answer · Individual · 10 min

Define logo design in your own words. What is the difference between a logo and a brand?

A client runs a law firm and asks for a logo that "looks modern and trendy." Using the five qualities, explain why "trendy" is a problem and what you would recommend instead.

Explain the difference between raster and vector graphics. Why must a logo be built as a vector file?

Describe the thumbnail sketching process. Why is it important to complete 20 thumbnails before refining any single concept?

A client receives their logo as a JPG only. List three specific problems this will cause and identify the correct formats that should have been delivered.

Part 5 · Section 6.4

Assignment: Brief to Delivery

Complete the full logo design process for one of the following briefs.

Brief A — Rooted

Local independent coffee shop. Values: community, sustainability, warmth, handcrafted quality. Audience: adults 25–45, urban, environmentally conscious.

Brief B — Spark

Children's after-school coding club. Values: curiosity, creativity, fun, learning. Audience: children 8–14 and their parents.

Brief C — Personal Brand

Freelance photographer's personal brand using their initials. Values: precision, artistry, professionalism. Audience: corporate and wedding clients.

Deliverables
  • Word Association List — 20–30 words; circle 5–8 most visual
  • Thumbnail Grid — 20 rough sketches (photographed or scanned)
  • Refined Sketches — 2–3 selected concepts at larger scale
  • Final Vector Logo — Full color + one-color black + one-color white
  • Written Reflection — 5–7 sentences: logo type, shape/line choices, color psychology, scalability confirmation
