Graphic Design · Module 03

Color Theory

    Ref. Mrantinkovic et al. (2020)
    3G Handy Guide: Graphic Designing
    Parts 5–6 · 25 min

    Group critique & wrap-up

    Three groups, one design sample each. 12 minutes to analyze, 2–3 minutes each to present. Tap a card to flip it for the discussion questions.

    Presentation format (2–3 min per group)Each group shares: (1) the design analyzed and palette identified, (2) the harmony type and psychological associations, (3) one strength and one weakness in the color use, (4) their proposed improvement or redesign.

    Key takeaways

    • Color theory is the systematic framework for how colors relate, interact, and communicate.
    • Four properties define every color: hue, value, saturation, temperature.
    • Six harmonies each carry a different energy: monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic, tetradic.
    • 60-30-10 is the go-to formula for applying any palette.
    • Psychology is a reliable tendency, not a universal law — culture always matters.
    • RGB is for screens, CMYK is for print — using the wrong one breaks consistency.
    • The most important element should carry the most dominant color treatment.

    Effective vs. ineffective color use

    FeatureEffectiveIneffective
    HarmonyColors chosen from a deliberate harmonyColors chosen randomly with no relationship
    HierarchyMost important element has the most dominant colorAll elements compete with equal color weight
    Proportion60-30-10 applied — one color clearly dominatesAll colors used in equal amounts
    PsychologyColor mood matches the design's intent and audienceColor mood contradicts the message (e.g., red for a spa)
    ContrastSufficient value contrast for readabilityLow contrast makes text hard to read
    ConsistencyPalette is consistent throughout the designColors change randomly between sections
    Cultural fitColors are appropriate for the target cultureColors carry unintended cultural meanings
    Media accuracyCorrect model used (RGB for screen, CMYK for print)Wrong model causes color shifts between media

    Self-check questions

    Define color theory in your own words — why is it foundational to graphic design?

    Explain hue vs. value vs. saturation, with one example of each building visual hierarchy.

    A children's party poster uses dark monochromatic navy. What's the problem, and what harmony fixes it?

    Why does 60-30-10 outperform using three colors in equal amounts?

    A design looks right on screen but dull once printed — what is the likely cause, and how do you prevent it?

    Assignment — build a color palette

    Pick a brief: cafe brand, fitness app, children's book cover, or luxury watch ad. Build Palette A (a harmony that deliberately clashes with the brief's mood) and Palette B (one that fits, run through 60-30-10). Apply Palette B to a mock-up and write a 5–7 sentence reflection on harmony, psychology, and proportion. Due next class.

    Evaluation criteria
    Palette A clearly demonstrates a color/mood mismatch with written explanation · Palette B uses a named, identifiable harmony · 60-30-10 is visibly applied in the mock-up · Written reflection accurately identifies harmony, psychology, and proportion · Mock-up demonstrates color hierarchy (most important element has most dominant color)
