    Harmonies & schemes

    A structured relationship between colors on the wheel that reads as visually intentional rather than accidental.

    Base hue 250°

    Monochromatic

    One hue, varied in value. Cohesive and elegant, but can feel flat. Chanel leans on black-and-white monochrome for timelessness.

    Best for: Minimalist designs, luxury branding.

    Analogous

    3–5 neighboring hues. Found constantly in nature — a sunset's yellow, orange, and red-orange is a textbook analogous palette.

    Best for: Nature themes, mood-driven illustrations.

    Complementary

    Two opposite hues at maximum contrast. The Lakers' purple and gold is a classic pair — one color should dominate at 60–70%.

    Best for: CTAs, sports branding, promotional materials.

    Split-complementary

    A base color plus the two neighbors of its complement. High contrast, less harsh — a gentler entry point for beginners.

    Best for: Designs needing energy with more sophistication.

    Triadic

    Three hues 120° apart. Vibrant and playful — Google's logo and most kids' brands live here.

    Best for: Children's media, entertainment, infographics.

    Tetradic

    Two complementary pairs forming a rectangle. The richest, hardest-to-balance scheme — needs one dominant color.

    Best for: Complex editorial layouts, multi-category branding.
