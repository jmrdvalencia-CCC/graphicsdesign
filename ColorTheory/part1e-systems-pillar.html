    🖨️ Color systems

    How color is produced and reproduced across different media — the reason a vivid screen color can look dull once printed, and how to prevent it.

    What it covers

    The technical models that define how color is created — either by combining light (additive) or by absorbing it with ink (subtractive). Graphic designers must understand both to ensure color consistency across all outputs.

    RGB — additive light

    Used for screens: monitors, phones, tablets, projectors. Colors are created by adding light — combining all three at full intensity produces white.

    R + G + B = White
    No light = Black

    CMYK — subtractive ink

    Used for print: offset printing, packaging, physical media. Colors are created by subtracting light — ink absorbs certain wavelengths.

    C + M + Y = Dark brown
    + K (black) = True black

    Critical ruleAlways design in RGB for screen and convert to CMYK for print. A color that looks vivid on screen may appear dull when printed if not properly converted. Always request a physical proof before final printing.
