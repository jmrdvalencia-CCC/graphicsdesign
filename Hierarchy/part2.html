2.1 — Size

Tool 1: Size

The most immediate and powerful tool of visual hierarchy. Try the presets on the right.

  • Larger elements are perceived as more important — this is instinctive.
  • A large headline immediately signals: "this is the most important thing here."
  • Differences must be significant to be effective — 20px vs. 22px is invisible; 20px vs. 48px is unmistakable.
  • Size hierarchy applies to images, shapes, icons, and text alike.
Real-World Example: A movie poster — the film title is always the largest text element, often 5–10× larger than the cast names below it.

2.2 — Color

Tool 2: Color

Color creates hierarchy through contrast, saturation, and psychological weight. Click a swatch to see its technique.

Click a Swatch
Accent Color: One vivid color on a neutral palette draws the eye. Example: a red "Buy Now" button on a white page.
Techniques
Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) advance visually — they feel closer and more urgent.
Cool colors (blue, green, purple) recede — they feel calmer and more secondary.
Desaturation — muting secondary elements makes the primary stand out.
Color coding — a consistent color per hierarchy level trains the viewer.
⚠️ Common Mistake: Using too many vivid colors at the same saturation level. Hierarchy requires restraint — use strong color for the most important element only.

Real-World Example: Apple product pages — a single product image centered on a vast white background says: "this is the only thing that matters."
Spacing Hierarchy Rules
  • More space around an element increases its perceived importance.
  • Less space between elements groups them as equal-level content.
  • Consistent spacing per level trains the viewer to recognize hierarchy levels.
  • Padding inside a container makes the content inside feel more important.

2.5 — Typography

Tool 5: Typography

The most nuanced tool for text-heavy designs. Click a level below to isolate it.

Six Variables
Size — larger = more important (64px vs. 16px)
Weight — bold = more important than regular or thin
Style — italic or ALL CAPS signals a different level
Font family — different typefaces signal different levels
Color — colored or high-contrast text stands out
Tracking — wide letter-spacing on labels signals a level
Rule: Never rely on size alone. Combine size + weight + color for a clear, unmistakable typographic hierarchy.

2.6 — Placement

Tool 6: Placement

Where an element sits on the page directly affects how important it feels. Compare the three reading patterns below.

F-Pattern
Eyes scan left-to-right across the top, then down the left side. Common in text-heavy pages, articles, and emails.
Z-Pattern
Top-left → top-right → diagonal → bottom-left → bottom-right. Common in simple layouts, landing pages, and posters.
Gutenberg Diagram
Attention is highest at top-left and bottom-right; lowest at top-right and bottom-left. Common in print layouts and advertisements.
💬 Discussion: Look at a website homepage. Where is the most important call-to-action placed? How does its placement reinforce its importance?
