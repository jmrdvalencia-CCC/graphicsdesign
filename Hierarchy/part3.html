Part 3 Group Activity: Design Critique & Redesign · 20 min
Mrantinkovic et al. (2020)
Activity Overview

Critique It. Redesign It. Present It.

Students split into 3 groups, each working with a different design sample. Every group applies the six tools from Part 2 to diagnose — and fix — a real hierarchy problem. Click a group below to jump straight to its brief.

Group 1
Poster — Weak Hierarchy
A community event flyer where everything is the same size and color. Diagnose the problem and sketch a 3-level fix.
Group 2
Website — Strong Hierarchy
A well-designed landing page. Map the levels, name the tools at work, and find the one weak spot.
Group 3
Magazine Cover Analysis
A fashion, news, or lifestyle cover. Rank the typographic levels and test them in greyscale.

Setup: Give each group a printed or projected sample. Assign one note-taker and one presenter per group before the timer starts.
Group 1

Poster with Weak Hierarchy

Sample A community event flyer where all text is the same size and color. 🔍 Click image to enlarge
Tasks
  1. 1Identify which element should be the most important (event name, date, or venue).
  2. 2List which of the six tools are missing or misused.
  3. 3Sketch or describe a redesign that establishes a clear 3-level hierarchy.
  4. 4Present: "The hierarchy problem was ___ and we fixed it by ___."
Guide Questions
💬 What is the viewer supposed to do after reading this flyer?
💬 Can you tell what the event is within 3 seconds?
💬 Which tool would have the biggest impact if applied?
Group 2

Website with Strong Hierarchy

Sample A well-designed landing page — National Book Store (nationalbookstore.com) 🔍 Click image to enlarge  ·  🌐 Visit nationalbookstore.com ↗
Tasks
  1. 1Map the hierarchy — identify at least 4 distinct levels of importance.
  2. 2For each level, identify which of the six tools are being used.
  3. 3Find one element where the hierarchy could be improved and explain why.
  4. 4Present: "The hierarchy works because ___ and could be improved by ___."
Guide Questions
💬 Where does your eye go first? Second? Third?
💬 Which tool is doing the most work in this design?
💬 Is there any element that feels out of place in the hierarchy?
Group 3

Magazine Cover Analysis

Sample A magazine cover (fashion, news, or lifestyle). 🔍 Click image to enlarge
Tasks
  1. 1Identify the masthead, cover line, and secondary text — rank them by hierarchy level.
  2. 2Analyze how typography alone creates the hierarchy (size, weight, color, style).
  3. 3Identify how placement reinforces the hierarchy (what is above the fold, what is centered).
  4. 4Present: "The typographic hierarchy is achieved through ___ and placement reinforces it by ___."
Guide Questions
💬 If you removed all color, would the hierarchy still be clear?
💬 How many typographic levels can you count?
💬 Does the image compete with or support the text hierarchy?
Wrap-Up

Presentation Format

2–3 minutes per group. Each group shares four things — in this order.

1
The Design They Analyzed
Show or describe the sample the group worked with.
2
Their Hierarchy Map
Drawn or described — which levels exist and in what order.
3
One Strength, One Weakness
A single clear win and a single clear problem — no more.
4
Their Proposed Improvement
What would change, and which of the six tools makes it happen.
Facilitator Tip: Keep each group to their fill-in-the-blank sentence first, then open the floor for one follow-up question from the class before moving on.
